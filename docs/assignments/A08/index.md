# A8 – Gears

## Objective
Students are tasked with designing a gear box for the specified gear and pinion set with given dimensions and constraints based on shaft diameter and journal bearing size. Students must first determine if the provided shaft diameters are sufficient for the loads applied and then select an appropriate journal bearing to fit into the gearbox.

## Analyze
For this gearbox project, we are given the following information: The gear ratio is 24:9, the output torque and rotational speed are 1.25 in-lbf and 1100rpm respectively, the input and output shaft diameters are .125in and .25in relative, and each  shaft is a length of 1 inch. The gear dimensions and specifications are also provided in the resources page through McMaster-Carr, the pinion gear being 0.417in in diameter with 18 teeth and the big gear being 1.04in in diameter with 48 teeth. From this information, we must corroborate that the predetermined shaft diameters are sufficient and then proceed with an appropriate journal bearing to hold the shaft in the gearbox. The journal bearing will be a running fit for the shaft while the bearing itself will be a press-fit into its respective slot in the gearbox.

## Decide
I begun my analysis by determining if the input shaft diameter was sufficient. For this, I had to use the gear ratio and given output information to derive the corresponding speed and torque the input shaft was experiencing. The equations for such were provided in the Machinery's Handbook 31st edition in the pages listed on the assignment for shafts. I then proceeded to follow the same process for the output shaft, with the exception of already having the information necessary to determine the ultimate torque value the shaft could withstand based on a predetermined allowable stress and its polar moment of inertia. The calculations shown further below demonstrate that the .125 inch input shaft diameter and the .250 inch output shaft diameter are, in fact, sufficient for the load that is being applied by the gears. Next, we had to determine what journal bearing size to use. For this segment of the assignment, I made my decision based on the diameter of the shaft and what the smallest length and housing diameter I could use was. From here, I selected the appropriate bearings from McMaster-Carr. The decision for the journal bearing size was made by looking up the appropriate minimum clearance from the fit tables in the Machinery's Handbook and selecting an appropriate size based on that. During this assignment, I learned how to determine the maximum torque a shaft can endure based on its polar moment of inertia and allowable stress either by restriction or convention, as well as how to pair shafts and fits with journal bearings and take all the individual information to create a bigger product designed to integrate the systems as a whole. I also learned how to make informed decisions on tolerances and different integrations of parts based on the constraints of a given problem and how to start from one part to making a whole assembly that will integrate all the information I previously derived.
