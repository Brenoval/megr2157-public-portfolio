In this second part of the assignment, a simple mechanical product will be chosen to evaluate and study its design and intended purpose both from its original patent and the system it is being used in at the time of observation.

The mechanical system chosen for this assignment will be a simple coil spring found commonly in automotive suspensions. In the automotive application, the main purpose of the coil spring assembly is to provide an equal and opposite force in response to changes in the vehicle ride height as a result of the external environment. As the spring is compressed, any energy applied to the assembly will be stored as elastic potential energy modeled by the equation -(1/2)ks^2, where k is the elastic coefficient of the designed coil spring relative to its shape, size, and material, and s^2 resembles the distance that the spring has been stretched or compressed squared. The elastic potential energy modeled by the equation is transformed into an equal but opposite direction kinetic energy to rebound the force it received. This model is validated by the conservation of energy present in the physics of moving objects and energy transfer.
![Coil Spring Assembly](32405.jpeg)
The circular geometry of the coil spring assembly allows for it to receive the adequate amount of force intended from that specific corner of the vehicle, as well as being able to exert force back evenly over the metal panel it is installed in. 
The patent number for this coil spring assembly is US10385940B2 and it was invented by Jun Yoshioka. The inventor designed the spring with adequate spacing and strength in the numerous coils to not only allow for the intended compression but also so that the design is able to deform and rebound in such a way that it transmits its stored energy back to the source that originally gave it elastic potential energy. I believe a system of magnetic forces that counteract each other with sufficient force could be an adequate replacement for this coil spring assembly since the magnetism, in theory, would be repulsive enough to rebound adequately and would be able to maintain a steady rest state at static equilibrium. Another possible replacement would be a semi-compressible fluid that is able to act in a similar fashion to a spring, where the fluid would be able to absorb the potential energy as elastic potential energy and then be able to exert it back to regain equilibrium within the system. In any case, either replacement would have to also be designed with sufficient surface area such that the received and transmitted forces are equally distributed to increase efficiency and reduce failure rate within the system due to concentrated stress.
