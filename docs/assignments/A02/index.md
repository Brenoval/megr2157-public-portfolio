# A2 – Truss Stress Analysis

## Objective

The objectives of this assignment include designing a truss made of a500 steel or similar material to withstand the stresses implemented by 2 forces at points C and D. We are also tasked with analyzing our designed truss with free-body diagrams and static equilibrium to determine the magnitude of the forces on both the beams and the pins that make up the structure to then calculate the required cross-sectional area with a predetermined safety factor. The estimated weight of the truss is also to be calculated and compared with a CAD model matching the original design.

## Analyze

To start the project, we must first decide what the load P will be, with the restraint of it being between 20 and 30 kN. The following are given: point A is a pin, point B is  roller, dimension 'a' is .4m and dimension 'b' is .3m. The points are also located as seen in the image:

## Decide

I chose a simple trapezoidal shape due to the fact that points C and D have an applied force in opposite directions. I also chose the applied force P to be 25kN as a good compromise between both design restraints. Both points A and B have similar angles towards each point and, with point C having an upwards force, point A should be able to counteract any moments caused in the clockwise direction with its extra support in the x direction, thus not requiring extra support within the truss.

Following along both pages, we can see the calculations ranging from the external pin reactions and moments to then calculating the internal forces of the truss. At first, I attempted to calculate the internal forces by "slicing" the truss into pieces but I ultimately decided to take a pin-by-pin approach and properly determine each force one at a time. Although this approach may have been lengthier, the pin-by-pin approach allowed me to better dissect the truss and its forces to determine the cross-sectional areas needed. After organizing all the forces in a table, I proceeded to take the largest force both for the truss member and the pin and separately calculate what my area would have to be to withstand the nature of the forces in the direction they are acting.
